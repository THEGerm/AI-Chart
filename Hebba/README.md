# Hebba Enthroned

A portrait drawn entirely in code, in the same simulated risograph technique as `Egypt/`. It shows Hebba as a princess of the New Kingdom (c. 1550 to 1069 BCE), seated on a gilded throne inside a finished Egyptian frame.

- **File:** `index.html`, **73905 bytes**. It is a single file with Canvas 2D only: no images, fonts, libraries or network requests beyond the page itself.
- **No background:** only the paper stock shows behind the figure and throne.
- **Controls:** manual only. Drag to pan (with inertia), wheel or pinch to zoom, `+` / `-` to zoom, arrow keys to pan, and `0` to fit the sheet. The camera never moves by itself.

## Costume levels

A switch in the top-left corner changes her dress. Her face, body and pose are identical in every level.

| Level | Name | What she wears |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| II | Everyday | Plain linen sheath with a simple tie, a string of faience beads with her crescent amulet, small gold hoops, bead bracelets, light kohl, papyrus sandals, no diadem |
| III | Court | Pleated linen gown with cape sleeves and red sash, broad collar, diadem with lotus and streamers, gold disc earrings, gold bracelets and armlets, kohl and malachite |
| IV | Regalia | Adds a gold modius crown with cobras, twin plumes and sun disc; a larger broad collar with falcon-head clasps; a feathered vulture-wing wrap; gold-threaded pleats; heavier jewellery; ceremonial makeup with a long kohl wing, strong malachite, deep red lips and cheeks |

Each level keeps its own drawing and tile cache, so switching back is instant.

## The four inks

The painter's palette of the period:

| Ink | Hex | Screen angle | Used for |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kohl Black | `#2a2629` | 45° | Line, hair, kohl |
| Red Ochre | `#c4482f` | 75° | Skin warmth, lips, sash, carnelian |
| Gold Ochre | `#e8aa34` | 0° | Gold, skin, throne |
| Egyptian Blue | `#245ca8` | 15° | Lapis and faience; overprinted on gold it gives the malachite green |

## Likeness

The face is traced in the reference photograph's own pixel space and mapped onto the sheet, so her measured proportions carry over:

- the eye spacing and the brow line;
- the broad smile with the upper teeth showing;
- the rounded cheeks and the laughter lines;
- the shape of the nose and chin;
- the full, curly hair.

Her head keeps the pose of the photograph, rolled toward her left shoulder and pitched slightly down, so the nose is seen a little from above. Each pass of the drawing was checked side by side against the photograph.

## Costume

- **Diadem:** a gold band set with carnelian and lapis rosettes, a lotus at the brow, and streamers falling through her curls. Her own hair is kept and the diadem sits on top of it.
- **Collar:** a broad collar (*wesekh*) of gold, lapis and carnelian with drop pendants. A gold crescent amulet hangs at its centre, echoing the crescent pendant she wears in the photograph.
- **Gown:** pleated linen with short cape sleeves and a red sash knotted at the waist.
- **Jewellery:** gold disc earrings, bracelets and armlets.
- **In her hand:** a blue lotus.
- **The throne:** gilded, with lion heads and lion legs and the lotus-and-papyrus knot of the Two Lands beneath the seat.

## Frame

- a block border of blue, red, gold and green panels;
- corner rosettes;
- a gilded torus roll bound in kohl and blue;
- a lotus frieze along the foot;
- the winged sun disc at the crown.
